When I [retired from PHINUG](https://jonlimjap.net/blog/10-quitting-unquietly) two years ago, my attention gradually shifted to the plastic scale modeling community. That wasn't a dramatic shift as someone might imagine -- I first joined [IPMS Philippines Bert Anido](https://www.facebook.com/ipmsphilippinesBA) 23 years ago, and I've been part of the scale modeling community, on and off, for more than two decades. I've had years when I didn't attend and nearly a decade when I didn't build scale models at all, but I kept in touch with my friends from the club.

However, things took a drastic turn when, in 2024, I decided to help out in the very first [Philippine Scale Model Show (PSMS)](https://philippinescalemodelshow.com/) to handle social media and photography. It was a massive show with no less than 600 scale model entries, and held in one of the largest malls in Manila. I was pulled into the organizing committee, by friends who were internationally recognized in the community: three of them had been judges in international competitions in Malaysia and Hong Kong; two of them have models displayed in the Tamiya factory museum in Japan, and the rest have won multiple awards in scale model competitions both local and foreign. 

In contrast, I've never ever won any award in a scale model competition. In fact, I didn't even have a scale model entry during the first year. I was happy doing social media for the event, and pointed out to myself that in contrast to my previous community where I was the lead and the mentor, here I was the "n00b". I was learning something new all the time, and it felt amazing.

However, a nagging feeling overcame me whenever someone would ask: "where is your entry?"

I didn't have any.

I was happy to just do social media for the show. It was fulfilling, and the inspiration pushed me to build an entry for PSMS 2025. I entered it but didn't win anything. Truth be told I was quite happy with the certificate of participation, and to be the "dumbest guy in the room" -- surrounded by and working with literal scale modeling legends. 

Coming off of the high of PSMS 2025, I actually finished two entries for PSMS 2026. I quickly finished a Bandai Millennium Falcon for my first entry, and was determined to put in an entry for the sparsely participated Rockets and Real Space category. I found on sale a 1/72 Revell V2 Rocket, with decal options for Aggregat 4, the first rocket that crossed the Karman Line -- considered as the boundary between Earth's atmosphere and outer space. I built the model: the rocket, the launch pad, the painstaking masking job, multiple retouches and corrections, for what was my first ever fully weathered build. I applied the lessons learned from workshops conducted by [Arcus Hobby Colours](https://arcuscolours.com/) and various YouTube tutorials the best I could.

I came into the Philippine Scale Model Show 2026 the same as the previous years: without expectations for my entry. Fully focused on documenting the nearly 700 models, I immediately got busy with taking photos and videos of the event. When the awarding ceremony came I set up my tripod for the video and settled in fully expecting to be offstage for the full ceremony. 

When my name was called I almost panicked. I quickly handed my camera to a fellow organizer who was at the bottom of the stage.

And just like that, after over 2 decades in the hobby, I received my first award in a scale model competition.

  <img src="/images/a4v2withmedal.jpg" alt="Revell Aggregat 4 - V2 Rocket with PSMS 2026 silver medal"/>

While I'm really proud of this build and this achievement, I still have a lot more skills to learn and a long way to go in this hobby. That said I am very grateful to be surrounded by people who absolutely excel in this craft, and to be learning from them. As I'm now entering my late 40s, it's refreshing to be able to demonstrate that I could still pick up new skills and learn new things not just in technology but in the tangible, artistic sphere of humanity, an area that is being threatened and overshadowed by a seemingly growing global obsession with AI.

More importantly though, I rekindled my love for a hobby that I didn't have the chance to master when I was younger.
